I am an experimental physicist working in fluid dynamics, interested in geophysical flows, which I study building idealised experiments to understand the underlying physics.
<!-- As an early-career fluid dynamicists, I am trying to diversify my interests to cover a wide range of fluid dynamics phaenomena.  -->

At this time, I am working with <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/quentinkriaa">Quentin Kriaa</a> on <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_pool">cold pools</a>, supported by a <a href="https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/david-crighton-fellowships-2026">David Crighton Fellowship</a>. 

During my PhD, I worked with <a href="https://www.shuisman.com/">Sander Huisman</a> and <a href="https://pof.tnw.utwente.nl/">Detlef Lohse</a> on the melting of moving objects. We worked on the melting of unconstrained, floating ice cylinders (see <a href="/projects/floatingCylinder/">here</a>), on the melting of ice spheres in high levels of turbulence (see <a href="/projects/sphere/">here</a>), and on the melting of a rotating ice cylinder. Additionally, together with <a href='https://pof.tnw.utwente.nl/people/profile/21'>Jacco Snoeijer</a> and <a href='https://pof.tnw.utwente.nl/people/profile/18'>Leen van Wijngaarden</a>, we investigated the lubricating effect of the meltlayer on a melting object, inspired by butter sliding on a hot pan. 

I was a fellow of the 2025 edition of the <a href='https://gfd.whoi.edu/'>GFD Program</a> hosted by the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, where I worked with <a href='https://www2.whoi.edu/staff/ccenedese/'>Claudia Cenedese</a> and <a href='https://www.whoi.edu/profile/jmcelwaine/'>Jim McElwaine</a> on the interactions and instabilities of two floating currents in a rotating environment.

My <i>forma mentis </i> is the one that I have acquired during my Physics studies: I strive to summarize observations using explanatory and predictive theories.  I aim to delve into fundamental processes, rather than into how these reveal themselves in incidental manifestations.
